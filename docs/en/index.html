claude-task-orchestrator

The AI drives. It calls a human for four things

Implementation, triage and progress tracking are the AI’s job. It is not waiting to be told what to do. When the work reaches something only a person can do, it moves the assignee and stops there.

Division of labour

A human owns four things

(a)Confirming requirements and designWhen the spec is thin, the AI drafts requirements and a proposal first
(b)Testing the running applicationThe AI never claims to have looked at a screen
(c)Code review and mergingThe AI opens the PR and stops. It never presses the button
(d)Talking to other peopleThe AI never lets its own words be read as the human’s

Everything else is the AI’s. Implementation, triage, status transitions, tracking progress, diagnosing failures, acting on review comments. It is not a thing you queue work for.

Objective

It optimises the human’s waiting time, not the AI’s throughput

Those two often point in opposite directions. The human being the bottleneck is the premise, not a problem to fix.

NOT THIS Maximise throughput

Starting four more implementations while three items already sit waiting for review does not make the system faster. It only grows the pile the human has to get through.

THIS Hand over less, get it back sooner

Keep the pile small and keep it moving. That is what actually shortens the loop.

The AI never chases. It earns the right to stay quiet by being able to answer “where are we?” completely, the moment it is asked. Not pushing work at you; being ready to lay all of it out when you ask.

Handoff

Hand off the assignee, not the status

Ownership is decided by the assignee and nothing else. If the assignee is the AI, the task is the AI’s whatever its status. So a human hands work back the same way: change the assignee, leave the status alone.

QuestionWaiting on confirmed requirements (a)
TestWaiting on a person to exercise the change (b)
In ReviewWaiting on review and merge (c)
WaitThe AI is stuck, or something external has to arrive

The status view answers by duty, not by count: not “3 items” but “2 need requirements, 1 needs review, 3 need testing”.

Status records progress, so it is never rolled back

A run that dies at PR creation still finished the implementation. Rolling it back to Todo erases that. The reason goes in a comment before the assignee changes — the assignee change is the signal that reaches the human, so the reason has to already be there when they open it.

WHAT HAPPENED A failure path used to move the status to Wait. The assignee stayed the AI, so the task remained the AI’s ball with the AI out of moves, and nobody could tell it had been handed over at all.

The AI is never allowed to conclude “this status can’t be mine”

The only statuses excluded are Backlog and the terminal ones, and that list is a denylist, not an allowlist. Forgetting an entry in an allowlist hides work. Forgetting one in a denylist only leaves it on the list.

WHAT HAPPENED An allowlist of “statuses the AI may hold” hid every Test issue assigned to the AI from the status view. The AI could not read the instructions waiting in the comments, read the situation as impossible, and kept handing the issue back.
Runtime

What keeps the autonomy running

Autonomy does not survive on good judgement alone. It fails by stopping without anyone noticing. That is the whole job of the code.

01Keep the session aliveChecks every 20 minutes. Recycles a perfectly healthy session once its context hits the ceiling
02Hand it toolsReading and writing the task source, notifications, sorting a rebase, reaping verification containers
03Read the state from outsideAnswers even when the AI is down. You want it most when things are broken, so it never goes through the AI
04Define what healthy meansNot “does it answer” but “is it patrolling”. Only the heartbeat tells a stall apart
healthy ≝ the session is listed,
          and is not in a terminal state,
          and its heartbeat is recent

The failure mode that matters for a resident process is not death but a stalled session. A session whose context has degraded still answers; it just stops patrolling. The process is still there, so a liveness check reports “healthy” forever.

85%Share of eight days of spend that went on re-reading context
6%Share that went on output over the same period
300kDefault context ceiling for recycling the resident session
400kDefault context ceiling for making a child hand over

That is why the trigger is context and not elapsed time. The cost of a turn is set by how much context it is carrying, and context never shrinks. The age limit exists only as a fallback for when context could not be measured.

Why prose

Where to draw the line does not fit in a conditional

“Do I ask a person about this, or decide it myself?” does not reduce to a state machine. So the judgement and the control flow live in 846 lines of prose, and the 6,520 lines of TypeScript do nothing but keep it running.

prompts/orchestrator.md
A child goes blocked when it is waiting on a human decision. This state does not change with time. The child just keeps waiting inside its session, invisible to the human. Unless you hand it over, it stays stopped and nobody finds out.

That is not documentation of the control flow. It is the control flow. Changing the behaviour means rewriting the paragraph.

Embedding

Take it as a submodule and layer on top

The core knows nothing about the repository that embeds it. You layer your prompts to add child kinds, and list the core’s jobs alongside your own in your dispatcher.

# prompt sources are layered; later wins
ORCHESTRATOR_PROMPT_SOURCE_DIRS=core/prompts,prompts
ORCHESTRATOR_REPO_DIR=/path/to/your-repo
{{childRules}}Builds the table of child kinds from each source’s child/rules.json
{{extraSections}}Appends your own sections to the end of the instruction file
{{coreDir}} / {{repoDir}}Points at the core’s CLIs and at your repository’s scripts separately
TaskProviderA ten-method interface, with Linear and ClickUp implementations included

Status names come from configuration too. Whatever you put in workflow.json is substituted into the prompts at render time, so a workspace that calls Test deploy & test reads correctly without touching the prose.

A development loop built on the premise that the AI is not the bottleneck. The bottleneck is the human’s four duties, and how little you have to hand over is the centre of the design.
